# Answering the questions

Log retention is the outcome for both questions. It is the natural log of average concurrent players in month 6 divided by the average in month 1. A value closer to zero means the game kept more of its launch activity. The median among the 1,822 included games is −1.432, about 24% of month-1 players remaining. The critic score is the Metacritic number on the Steam page. The early player score is the share of positive Steam reviews in the first 30 days. "Better" means the larger absolute Spearman correlation with log retention: the score whose ranking lines up more closely with the retention ranking.

**Question 1.** On the 447 games that have both scores, the critic correlation is 0.136 (p = 0.004) and the early player correlation is 0.230 (p < 0.001). Early player scores are the better predictor. The match is still weak, and it does not hold for all 1,822 included games: there the early player correlation is 0.018 (p = 0.453). Most included games have no Metacritic score, so the win applies to games critics actually covered.

**Question 2.** Hybrid games are left out. Among 1,041 single-player games the early player correlation is 0.114 (p < 0.001). Among 163 multiplayer games it is −0.034 (p = 0.669). Yes: the score is a better predictor for single-player games than for multiplayer games, and both matches are small.

These are associations, not causes. Concurrent players are not the same people returning, and the sample is notable games with at least 1,000 lifetime reviews.
